## 📖 About the Project

Data visualization is widely used to simplify complex information and support decision-making across industries. However, visual representations are not always neutral. Design elements such as colour schemes, chart types, axis scaling, and layout can unintentionally—or deliberately—distort interpretation and influence human judgment.

This research explores how visualization design impacts perception, cognitive bias, and decision-making. The study highlights how misleading visuals can affect organizations, particularly in high-stakes sectors such as healthcare, finance, education, and manufacturing.

### ✨ Key Features

- 📊 Visualization Analysis — Examines how chart design affects interpretation
- 🧠 Cognitive Bias Study — Focus on framing effect, confirmation bias, and perception
- ⚖️ Ethical Visualization — Promotes transparency and responsible data presentation
- 🏢 Industry Survey — Research conducted across multiple organizations and sectors
- 📈 Decision-Making Impact — Evaluates how misleading visuals influence organizational decisions
- 📚 Data Literacy Awareness — Encourages critical evaluation of visual information

---

## 🧪 Research Methodology

1. Research Design — Descriptive and analytical research approach
2. Survey Collection — Data gathered from 10 organizations across various industries
3. Visualization Comparison — Same datasets presented using different chart styles and scales
4. Participant Evaluation — Respondents interpreted visuals and rated confidence levels
5. Data Analysis — Quantitative and qualitative analysis performed using statistical methods
6. Impact Assessment — Measured influence of visual design on interpretation and decisions
7. Ethical Evaluation — Studied awareness of misleading visualizations and ethical guidelines

## 📈 Results and Discussion

The study demonstrates that data visualization significantly influences interpretation and decision-making. Even when the underlying data remains accurate, design choices such as truncated axes, exaggerated colours, inappropriate chart types, and misleading layouts can alter perception and confidence levels.

The findings also reveal a gap between awareness and implementation. While many organizations understand the risks associated with misleading visuals, only a limited number follow ethical visualization standards or provide formal employee training.

Industries involving critical decision-making processes were found to be the most vulnerable to visualization-driven misinterpretation.
